Why a mechanic dad made this

I fix cars for a living. I’m also a dad of teen drivers.

When my own kids started driving, I realized they didn’t know what I check without thinking. Is that tire low? Is that puddle under the car new? Is a brake light out?

So I wrote down the exact walkaround I want my own kids to run before they pull out. Plain words, no tools but a cheap tire gauge, and a clear answer every time: drive it or park it.

What’s inside

  • Tires, first, every time. Check pressure with a gauge against the door sticker, and do the penny test. They know when a tire is fine and when it’s a park-it.
  • Lights. Headlights, brake lights, turn signals, reverse. If they can’t be seen, they don’t leave.
  • Glass and wipers. Cracks in their view, wipers that chatter, an empty washer bottle. Clear view before every drive.
  • A look under the car. What a fresh drip’s color means, and which ones mean don’t drive today.
  • The text-me rule. If something feels off, they text you before they pull out. You hear about it in the driveway, not from the shoulder.
  • Your Sunday 5 minutes, plus a stop-now or book-this-week page. Sort noises and warning lights into a clear lane, with no scare talk.
