Why a mechanic dad made this

I fix cars for a living. I’m also a dad of teen drivers.

When it was time to find a first car for my own kid, I did what I always do: a slow walkaround, a cold start, a real test drive, and a shop inspection before any money changed hands. Most parents don’t know what to look for, so they end up hoping the seller is honest.

So I wrote it down. One checklist for buying the car. One for checking it before every drive. Plain words and a clear answer every time.

What’s inside

1. Used Car Pre-Buy Checklist (5 pages)

  • Before you go. The questions to ask by phone, running a VIN history report, checking open recalls on NHTSA.gov, and looking up safety ratings on IIHS and NHTSA.
  • The 20-minute walkaround. Tire wear and tire age, panel gaps and paint, rust, leaks, under the hood with the engine cold, a cold start, lights, and glass.
  • Inside and the test drive. Warning lights at key-on, flood signs, odometer vs wear, then brakes, steering, shifting, noises, and highway speed.
  • The verdict page. Every finding goes in a lane: walk away, negotiate, or fine. Then get an independent pre-purchase inspection before you pay.
  • What a mechanic dad steers you away from. Why I trade a little fuel economy for reliability, and a table of engines and transmissions to research, based on public recalls, extended warranties, and class-action settlements.

2. Teen Driver Parent Checklist (3 pages)

  • The 90-second loop. Tires first (pressure and the penny test), lights, glass and wipers, and a look under the car before every drive.
  • Your Sunday 5 minutes. Tread wear, the spare, the brake pedal, warning lights, and smells.
  • Stop now or book this week. Noises and warning lights sorted into a clear lane, with no scare talk.
